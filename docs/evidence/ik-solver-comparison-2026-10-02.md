# IK solver comparison for the pick pipeline's Cartesian moves (2026-10-02)

Evidence for issue #38 ("Cartesian planning: pick_ik fails at 1 mm steps on the real
description -- re-test ur7e_motion primitives"). Raw per-call data:
`ik-solver-comparison-2026-10-02.json` (14 runs, 11 580 `/compute_cartesian_path` calls);
every table below is regenerated by
`python3 scripts/ik_solver_comparison.py --report docs/evidence/ik-solver-comparison-2026-10-02.json`.

**This is not the physical arm.** It is ros2_control mock hardware serving the robot's
calibrated description, with synthetic encoder noise, on a quiet 2-CPU container. What it
can say is how the two solvers behave on *our* kinematics for *our* requests; what it cannot
say is anything about the real controller, the real scene or the real laptop load.

## Summary

1. From the `ready` pose, with the kinematics yaml as committed, **pick_ik and KDL both plan
   every feasible request 30/30**, at 1, 2.5, 5 and 10 mm steps. The lab's "100 % or 4 %
   depending on the start sample" did not reproduce on a quiet machine (1680 calls per
   solver, plus 1680 more for pick_ik with the old 0.05 s timeout).
2. **The pre-retune yaml reproduces the lab failures exactly**: `minimal_displacement_weight:
   1.0` plans every lateral move to 2-4 % (0/30), descents to 10/30 and 2/30 depending on the
   noise, and nothing at all at >= 2.5 mm steps. Changing only the timeout does not help
   (identical counts at 0.05 s and 0.5 s); changing only the weight fixes it.
3. The weight 0.001 + 0.05 s combination breaks again when move_group is starved of CPU
   (23-29/30), while weight 0.001 + 0.5 s and KDL (0.05 s) stay at 30/30 under the same
   stress. The lab's flip is consistent with a time-boxed solve on a busy laptop.
4. **Path accuracy separates the solvers**: KDL follows the requested line to within 0.01 mm
   (0.06 mm at 10 mm steps) with no tool rotation; pick_ik's accepted paths wander up to
   0.98 mm off the line, end up to 1.0 mm off target and tilt the tool up to 3.3 mrad --
   its own `position_threshold` / `orientation_threshold`, by construction.
5. Two things that were believed are not true on this MoveIt build (2.5.10): the request's
   `revolute_jump_threshold` is ignored (0.00001 planned the same 30/30 as 0.02), and
   `max_step` does not have to be 1 mm -- that limit was a weight-1.0 artefact.

**Recommendation (checklist 2 and 3): KDL for every Cartesian segment; keep the retuned
yaml values for the pick_ik group that remains for grasp IK.** Details and the concrete
changes are in the last section.

### Path quality of the complete paths

Worst case over the 30 complete paths of a cell: largest joint travel along the path (rad) ·
largest distance of tool0 from the requested straight line (mm) · end-point error (mm) ·
largest tool rotation away from the start orientation (mrad). Full tables for every run are
in the `--report` output; this is the pattern, which holds in every row:

| solver, max_step | line deviation, max (median) | end error, max (median) | rotation, max (median) |
|---|---|---|---|
| kdl, 1 mm | 0.002 mm (0.001) | 0.003 mm (0.001) | 0.00 mrad |
| kdl, 2.5 mm | 0.009 mm (0.007) | 0.009 mm (0.007) | 0.00 mrad |
| kdl, 10 mm | 0.056 mm (0.035) | 0.000 mm | 0.00 mrad |
| pick_ik, 1 mm | 0.98 mm (0.57) | 1.00 mm (0.88) | 2.99 mrad (1.76) |
| pick_ik, 2.5 mm | 0.98 mm (0.63) | 1.00 mm (0.85) | 3.14 mrad (1.69) |
| pick_ik, 10 mm | 1.00 mm (0.78) | 1.00 mm (0.87) | 3.25 mrad (1.65) |
| pick_ik, position_threshold 0.0002, 1 mm | 0.19 mm (0.14) | 0.20 mm (0.18) | 0.73 mrad (0.33) |

(390 complete paths per row; the KDL 10 mm deviation is interpolation between exact waypoints.)
pick_ik stops each step as soon as the pose is inside `position_threshold` (1 mm) and
`orientation_threshold` (0.01 rad), so its "100 %" path is a walk inside a 1 mm tube that
ends up to 1 mm from the target and tilts the tool up to 0.19 degrees. KDL solves each step
to numerical precision. The joint travel of the complete paths is the same for both solvers
(e.g. 0.13 rad for a 5 cm descent, 0.86 rad for 30 cm): neither flips the wrist on a
complete path from `ready`.

On the *infeasible* request the solvers differ in the other direction: KDL's partial
`x_plus_20cm` paths (fraction 0.55-0.66) carry 3.1 to 15.7 rad of joint travel (median 9.4;
the arm would spin through the singularity on the way), pick_ik's stop cleanly at 0.26 rad.
Both are rejected by the `fraction >= 0.999` gate -- which is the *only* thing rejecting them,
see "jump threshold" below.

## Checklist 3: the yaml retune, isolated

Committed values since 2026-09-30 (`bb938f0`): `kinematics_solver_timeout: 0.5`,
`kinematics_solver_attempts: 5`, `minimal_displacement_weight: 0.001`. Before (`4899524`):
0.05 / 3 / 1.0. Each combination was run through the same requests and noise samples.

| run (pick_ik) | weight | timeout | feasible cells at 30/30 | cells below 30/30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `pick_ik_pre_retune` | 1.0 | 0.05 s | 4 of 52 | 48: Y moves 0/30 at 1 mm (fractions 0.02-0.04); descend_5cm 10/30, descend_15cm 2/30, lift_5cm 12/30, lift_15cm 1/30, descend_30cm 0/30; **every** request 0/30 at 2.5, 5 and 10 mm; only the X moves and the diagonal survive, at 1 mm |
| `pick_ik_w1.0_t0.5` | 1.0 | 0.5 s | 1 of 14 | 13, with **identical** counts to the row above (10/30, 2/30, 12/30, 0/30 ...): the failure is deterministic in the start state and the solve budget plays no part |
| `pick_ik_w0.001_t0.05` | 0.001 | 0.05 s | 52 of 52 | none on a quiet CPU |
| `pick_ik` (committed) | 0.001 | 0.5 s | 52 of 52 | none |
| `pick_ik_w0.001_t0.05+cpu_contention` | 0.001 | 0.05 s | 3 of 14 | 11: 23-29/30 (worst fraction 0.00) when four busy loops share the container's 2 CPUs with move_group |
| `pick_ik+cpu_contention` | 0.001 | 0.5 s | 14 of 14 | none under the same stress |
| `kdl+cpu_contention` | - | 0.05 s | 14 of 14 | none under the same stress |

Reading: the weight retune is what makes pick_ik plan Cartesian paths at all on this
description (`motion_node._configure_ik` also overrides the weight to 0.001 at runtime after
an approach, which is why the pipeline could work in sim with 1.0 in the file). The timeout
retune buys nothing on an idle machine and everything on a starved one:
pick_ik's local gradient descent is boxed in wall-clock time, so a scheduler stall during a
step costs the step. The stress test freezes move_group for part of every 100 ms scheduling
period; it is a mechanism demonstration, not a replica of the lab laptop.

Other pick_ik parameters, measured because the code or the lab touched them:

* `cost_threshold 0.01` (what `_configure_ik('local')` sets after an approach): all 56 cells
  identical to 0.001, path for path. Harmless.
* `position_threshold 0.0002` (the lab's "0 % everywhere"): 27/30 and 29/30 on `y_plus_20cm`,
  30/30 elsewhere, with 0.05 s or 0.5 s; accuracy improves 5x (table above). Not the 0 %
  seen in the lab, but not free either: the three failures stopped at 0.055-0.896.

## What did and did not reproduce from 2026-09-30

| Lab observation | Here |
|---|---|
| weight 1.0: lateral 5 cm paths reach 2 % from every pose, Z/X reach 100 % | **Reproduced** for Y (0/30, fractions 0.02-0.04). Z did not fully reproduce: descents flip 10/30 with +/-0.0005 rad noise; X 30/30 |
| weight 0.001: the same move flips between 100 % and 4 % with the start sample | **Not reproduced** on a quiet CPU (3360 calls over the 0.05 s and 0.5 s runs; every feasible cell 30/30). Reproduced only when move_group is starved and the timeout is 0.05 s |
| position_threshold 0.0002: 0 % everywhere | **Not reproduced**: 27-30/30 |
| max_step 2 mm -> 9 %, 5 mm -> 0 % | **Reproduced only with weight 1.0** (0/30 for every request at 2.5 mm). With weight 0.001 or with KDL, 2.5-10 mm steps are 30/30 |
| KDL 18/18 lateral at 100 % | Reproduced (30/30 everywhere feasible, also under CPU stress) |

What is different from the lab and could explain the two non-reproductions: (a) the lab's
flips were observed before `ready` existed, from the folded resting pose and its
neighbourhood, which sits on the shoulder singularity -- `ready` was chosen precisely so
that 5 cm moves plan in all directions; (b) the lab laptop ran the real driver's 500 Hz
loop, the console, voice input and more beside move_group, and the solve is time-boxed;
(c) real encoder samples instead of uniform +/-0.0005 rad; (d) the 0.0002 tightening in the
lab may have been tried while the weight was still 1.0 (the session log does not say). (a)
is the most likely: with weight 1.0 the mock setup reproduces the lab's lateral failure
digit for digit, so the setup is faithful where it can be compared.

## Also found

* **`revolute_jump_threshold` is ignored** by this move_group. `kdl_revolute_jump_1e-5` sends
  0.00001 rad -- smaller than any single IK step -- and plans 30/30 identical paths; the
  capability's log line prints only the relative `jump_threshold`, which both the console and
  pymoveit2 leave at 0 (off). Nothing in the request rejects a joint jump; the comment in
  `lab_arm_moveit.launch.py` ("revolute_jump_threshold still guards the wrist singularities")
  describes a guard that is not there. `lab_jog.audit` catches large excursions before
  executing; `ur7e_motion` has no such check.
* **A 30 cm descent from `ready` collides**: with the console's table box the RG2 envelope
  meets the table after 22.7 cm (fraction 0.751, both solvers). Grasp heights the pipeline
  can reach from `ready` start about 27 cm above the base plane for `tool0`.

## Recommendation

**Checklist 2 -- solver for the pick pipeline: KDL for every Cartesian segment
(`cartesian_descend`, `cartesian_lift`, `retreat`, and the descent check inside
`approach_above`); pick_ik only for the grasp IK in `approach_above`, which this measurement
did not cover.** How strongly the data supports it:

* Reliability does not decide it: at `ready`, both solvers are 30/30 with the committed yaml
  (strong, 1680 paired calls each).
* Accuracy decides it: KDL follows the line to 0.01 mm, pick_ik to 1 mm with up to 3.3 mrad
  of tool tilt -- a direct consequence of its accept thresholds, so this will hold on the
  arm (strong). For a top-down grasp a millimetre of lateral wander during the descent and a
  millimetre short at the bottom are tolerable but unnecessary; the console already pays
  nothing for KDL's exactness.
* Fragility decides it too: pick_ik's Cartesian result hinges on a tuning weight (1.0 ->
  0/30) and on wall-clock budget under load (0.05 s -> 23-29/30); KDL with 0.05 s stayed
  30/30 in the same stress test (strong for the mechanism, moderate as a prediction of the
  lab laptop).
* pick_ik's advantage is in the one thing *not* measured here: picking an IK branch for a new
  grasp pose far from the seed (`mode: global`, D6). Keep it there until that is measured;
  do not use it to step along a line.

Concretely (describe only -- none of this is changed here):

1. `src/ur7e_pick_place_bringup/srdf` + `config/ur7e_kinematics.yaml`: add a second planning
   group over the same chain and tip, e.g. `ur_onrobot_manipulator_cartesian`, with
   `kinematics_solver: kdl_kinematics_plugin/KDLKinematicsPlugin`,
   `kinematics_solver_search_resolution: 0.005`, `kinematics_solver_timeout: 0.05` (the
   console's values). MoveIt binds one solver per group, so two solvers means two groups.
   Keep `ur_onrobot_manipulator` on pick_ik for `compute_ik`.
2. `src/ur7e_motion/ur7e_motion/motion_node.py`: a second `pymoveit2.MoveIt2` bound to the
   KDL group; `primitives._cartesian_z_move` and the descent check in `approach_above` plan
   through it (`move_to_pose(..., cartesian=True)`), `compute_ik` stays on the pick_ik one.
3. `max_step` for those segments: anything from 1 to 10 mm planned 30/30 with KDL; the
   pymoveit2 default of 2.5 mm (0.009 mm line deviation here) is fine, and 5 mm halves the
   planning time again. There is no reason to force 1 mm.
4. Add the excursion audit the console has (`lab_jog.audit`: refuse a path whose largest
   joint excursion exceeds a bound) before executing any Cartesian path in `ur7e_motion`,
   because no jump threshold in the request is honoured on this build. KDL's partial paths
   through a singularity showed why (up to 15.7 rad of travel in a 66 % path).
5. Correct the two comments now known to be wrong: `lab_arm_moveit.launch.py` on
   `revolute_jump_threshold`, and `lab_jog.plan_move` / RUNBOOK finding 2 on "`max_step` must
   be 1 mm" (true for pick_ik at weight 1.0, not in general).

**Checklist 3 -- the retuned yaml values: keep both.** `minimal_displacement_weight: 0.001`
is the difference between 0/30 and 30/30 on every lateral move and on every move at >= 2.5 mm
steps (deterministic, 1680 calls each way). `kinematics_solver_timeout: 0.5` is the
difference between 30/30 and 23-29/30 when move_group is starved; it costs nothing when the
solve is quick (planning times are identical to 0.05 s on an idle machine). If pick_ik keeps
any Cartesian duty, `position_threshold: 0.0002` is worth a measured retry on the arm (5x
better accuracy here at the price of 1-in-10 failures on the 20 cm lateral move), and the
yaml comment that it "never converged at all" should be softened to what was measured.
`kinematics_solver_attempts` (3 -> 5) was not isolated; MoveIt 2 deprecates it in favour of
the timeout, so it is unlikely to matter either way.

## Not measured

* The physical arm: real encoder samples, the real controller, the real planning scene.
* Any start pose other than `ready` and its two lowered states -- in particular the folded
  resting pose on the shoulder singularity, where the lab's flips were most likely seen.
* The pick pipeline's own MoveIt configuration: `ur7e_pick_place_bringup` loads the full
  `ur_onrobot` description (RG2 links, group `ur_onrobot_manipulator`, gripper tip). This
  measurement uses the arm-only console description with the RG2 collision envelope and
  `tool0`, because that is what runs on the real arm today and what the issue's lab numbers
  came from.
* `approach_above`'s grasp IK (pick_ik `global` mode) -- the primitive where pick_ik's
  branch selection is the point. Nothing here says whether KDL could replace it.
* A busy lab laptop: the stress test shows the mechanism (time-boxed solve + scheduler
  stall), not the lab's actual load.

## Reproduce

```
  -e ROS_LOCALHOST_ONLY=1 -v $PWD:/workspaces/AI_arm -w /workspaces/AI_arm \

ros2 launch ur7e_bringup ur7e_bringup.launch.py use_mock_hardware:=true launch_rviz:=false &

python3 scripts/ik_solver_comparison.py --report out.json
```

The script refuses `ROS_DOMAIN_ID=42` and refuses to run against anything but exactly one
`move_group`. The pick_ik variants were run from container-local copies of
`lab_arm_moveit.launch.py` next to an edited `ur7e_kinematics.yaml` (the launch file reads
the yaml relative to its own path), or by `ros2 param set /move_group
robot_description_kinematics.ur_manipulator.<name>` for the dynamic parameters; each run's
`kinematics_parameters` block in the JSON is what move_group reported, not what was intended.
